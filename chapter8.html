Chapter 8

Introduction to Information Security
Information security refers to the comprehensive practice of protecting digital data, hardware, and software 
systems from unauthorized access, disruption, modification, or destruction. In an increasingly hyper-connected 
digital landscape, safeguarding sensitive information is vital for personal privacy, financial stability, and 
national security. The foundation of information security rests upon the CIA Triad: Confidentiality, which ensures 
sensitive data is accessible only to authorized individuals; Integrity, which guarantees data accuracy and prevents 
unauthorized tampering; and Availability, which ensures systems and data are reliably accessible to legitimate users 
whenever needed.

Common Security Threats and Vulnerabilities
Computer systems face a wide array of cyber threats engineered by malicious actors or triggered by accidental 
vulnerabilities. Malware—encompassing viruses, worms, Trojans, and ransomware—is malicious software designed to 
infiltrate, damage, or extort systems. Phishing attacks utilize deceptive emails, fraudulent websites, or social 
engineering techniques to trick individuals into revealing sensitive credentials, passwords, or financial information. 
Other significant threats include denial-of-service (DoS) attacks that overwhelm network resources, password cracking, 
and zero-day exploits targeting undocumented software flaws.

Security Measures and Defense Mechanisms
To mitigate security risks, organizations and individuals must deploy a robust, multi-layered defense architecture. 
Cryptography plays a critical role by transforming plaintext into unreadable ciphertext through encryption algorithms, 
requiring secure keys for decryption. Firewalls act as strict gatekeepers between internal networks and external 
traffic, filtering data packets based on predefined security rules. Additionally, intrusion detection systems (IDS), 
robust multi-factor authentication (MFA), regular data backups, and comprehensive antivirus software form an essential 
shield against cyber intrusions.

Concept of Cyber Law and Ethics
Cyber law encompasses the legal framework and legislative statutes governing the digital world, addressing legal issues 
related to internet usage, electronic commerce, software intellectual property, and cybercrimes. Because technology 
evolves faster than traditional legal boundaries, cyber laws are essential to prosecute digital offenses like hacking, 
identity theft, data breaches, and online harassment. In Nepal, the Electronic Transactions Act (ETA) regulates 
electronic records, digital signatures, and outlines legal penalties for cybercrimes. Alongside cyber law, digital 
ethics emphasizes responsible computer usage, respecting user privacy, avoiding plagiarism, and upholding moral 
standards in online communities.
